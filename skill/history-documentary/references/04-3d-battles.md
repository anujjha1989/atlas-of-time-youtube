# 4. Realistic 3D battle reconstruction

The battle is a real-time three.js scene (React Three Fiber via `@remotion/three`) rendered frame by frame by
Remotion. The progression that worked, and why each earlier stage was rejected by the owner:

1. Primitive shapes (spheres, boxes): "too sketchy".
2. Detailed hand-built low-poly models: better, but still obviously toys up close.
3. **Real models + photographic environment**: Sketchfab elephants, horses and soldiers; Poly Haven sky (HDRI),
   PBR ground, trees and grass. This is the standard to aim for.

## 4.1 Files (template/src/battle)

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `Battle3D.tsx` | The scene: `BParams` state, army layout, instanced figures, carts, guns, town, ditch, camp, riders, arrows, dust, banners, lights |
| `real.tsx` | Loads the real models, normalises them (scale, facing, feet at y=0), the war elephant with fitted gear, the seated-rider cut, the animated horse |
| `env.tsx` | HDR sky (also the lighting), PBR ground, grass overlay, scattered vegetation |
| `assets.ts` | Asset cache plus `preloadBattle()`, which loads everything **before** the canvas mounts (see 4.6) |
| `models.tsx` | Procedural props: helmets, shields, spears, bows, saddle cloths, the coat, caparison and howdah geometry, banners |
| `scenes/Visuals.tsx` → `battleShot(v, f, dur, s)` | Per-beat camera keyframes and `BParams` (what is visible, how far armies have moved) |
| `scenes/ModelTest.tsx` | "Models" composition: every model side by side, used to check scale and facing |
| `scenes/Thumb.tsx` | Thumbnail compositions that reuse the battle scene |

## 4.2 Coordinates and scale

- World: x = east, z = south, y = up. For Panipat, Babur's cart line is along z = 0, Ibrahim's army starts at
  z ≈ 115, the town is to the west (x < −120), and the camera looks south.
- **Layout units** are compressed (1 unit ≈ 10 m between blocks) so a 1.5 km battlefield fits on screen, but
  **figures are true size in their own units** (a man is 1.75, a horse 1.75 to the ears, an elephant 3.0 at
  the top of the back). This mixed scale reads naturally on camera; keep it.
- Army blocks are generated by `blockFigs(cx, cz, cols, rows, side, horse, seed)`; positions are jittered with
  `random(seed)` (Remotion's deterministic random), so every render is identical.

## 4.3 State per beat (`BParams`)

`battleShot()` returns `{ p, tags }` for a visual id: the camera (`cam: {pos, look, fov}` interpolated with
`camLerp(f, keys)`), and 0–1 controls: `carts`, `mantelets`, `guns`, `town`, `ditch`, `baburCentre`, `wings`,
`flank` (progress of the flanking sweep along a quadratic curve), `lodi` (visibility), `lodiZ` (advance),
`crush` (compression), `arrows`, `fire`, `camp`, `raiders`, `scouts` (−1 off, else progress), `night`, `dawn`,
`defeat`, and `t` (seconds, for idle motion). Drive them from `f` and the sentence times `s`, so things
happen when the narrator says them.

**Never use `useFrame`**: all motion must be a pure function of `useCurrentFrame()`, or frames render
differently in parallel tabs.

## 4.4 Real models

- `useReal(key)` returns normalised parts (one geometry per material, transforms baked, centred on x/z, feet
  at y = 0, facing +z, at the target height in `FIX[key].h`). Facing corrections live in `FIX[key].yaw`.
  The Sketchfab elephant needed `yaw: Math.PI`; check every new model in the **Models** composition.
- **Crowds are instanced**: one `InstancedMesh` per material part, with per-instance matrices written in a
  `useLayoutEffect`. The soldier crowd uses the simplified warrior (2.4k triangles); ~3,600 instances render
  fine on an M2 Pro. Set `frustumCulled={false}` on instanced meshes, because the bounding sphere is the base mesh.
- **Per-instance colour** (`instanceColor`) multiplies the texture: use light washes (`#d7e8dc` vs
  `#ffe3d2`) to separate sides without painting skin green or red.
- **Riders**: `riderParts(man, 0.5)` removes every triangle below the knee and shifts the model down; place
  the rider at y = 0.9 on a horse whose back is at ~1.3. Lower legs would otherwise poke through the belly.
- **Animated horse**: `RiggedHorse` clones the skinned scene (`SkeletonUtils.clone`) and calls
  `mixer.setTime(t)` every frame, which is deterministic. Use it only for small close groups (raiders,
  scouts). Crowds use the static pose.
- **War elephant**: the real elephant plus procedural gear (cloth over the back, howdah with gilded canopy,
  two riders), scaled to the measured back height (`bbox.max.y × 0.94`).

## 4.5 Historical accuracy (check before rendering)

Look at each close shot and ask "would a historian wince?". Panipat fixes:

- The first free cannon (Poly Haven `cannon_01`) was an 18th-century naval gun on a truck carriage, which is
  **wrong for 1526**. Replaced with a field gun: bronze barrel with reinforcing rings on a wooden bed.
- The free soldier model is bare-chested. Babur's Central Asian troops wore long coats and steel helmets, so
  **all** of Babur's men get a coat (the procedural `S.body` tunic, slightly scaled to fit) and most get a
  pointed helmet. About half of Ibrahim's infantry stay bare-chested (plausible for Indian foot soldiers);
  the rest wear coats.
- Elephants carry caparisons and howdahs; banners per block; tents in the Lodi camp.
- Dress, weapons, armour and fortifications differ by century and region, so research them like facts.

## 4.6 Environment

- **Sky** (`Sky`): an equirectangular HDR is both `scene.background` and `scene.environment` (image-based
  lighting), with ACES filmic tone mapping. Exposure 0.72 by day; `backgroundIntensity` 0.85.
  `kloofendal_48d_partly_cloudy_puresky` (blue sky, clouds) for day, `qwantani_sunrise_puresky` for dawn,
  `qwantani_night_puresky` for night. The plain morning HDRI looked washed-out white; prefer skies with
  visible blue and clouds.
- **Haze**: `FogExp2` with density 0.0009 by day and a colour close to the horizon. Too dense and the sky
  goes milky; too thin and the edge of the ground shows.
- **Ground** (`Ground`): a 6000 × 6000 plane with `dry_ground_01` diffuse, normal and roughness tiled 1100×,
  plus a large-scale variation texture in `aoMap` (hides tiling), and a second alpha-masked plane of
  `aerial_grass_rock` that thins out over the trampled battlefield.
- **Vegetation** (`Vegetation`): Poly Haven trees, grass clumps, boulders and logs scattered with a seeded
  random, kept off the battlefield, the town and the camp. Heights are normalised from each model's bounding box.
- **Lights**: one directional "sun" with 4096² soft shadows (`shadows="soft"`, bias −0.0004, normalBias 0.04)
  aligned roughly with the HDRI's sun; no ambient or hemisphere lights, since the HDRI does that.

## 4.7 Remotion and three.js rules learned the hard way

- **Preload in the outer React tree.** `delayRender()` called inside the r3f canvas (a separate React root) did
  not hold the frame, and stills came out with no sky or ground. Fix: `Battle3D` calls
  `useAsset("battle-assets", preloadBattle)` and returns `null` until loaded; inner components read the cache.
- **Post-processing doesn't work through Remotion's renderer.** `@react-three/postprocessing`'s
  `EffectComposer` (N8AO, Bloom) produced **blank frames**. Get depth from soft shadows, haze and image-based
  lighting instead.
- **Old Sketchfab materials**: convert with `gltf-transform metalrough` (KHR_materials_pbrSpecularGlossiness
  was removed from three.js).
- `mergeGeometries` returns `null` if parts have mismatched attributes or indexing: make every part
  non-indexed with only `position` and `normal` before merging (see `place()` in `models.tsx`).
- Heavy scenes leak GPU contexts over a long render. **Render in chunks** (reference 06).

## 4.8 Shot list that worked (Panipat)

`lodi-host` (low pan along the elephant line), `babur-army` (high, slow push), `archers` (riders galloping out
and back, camera tracking), `field-carts` (the cart line assembling, labels for carts and mantelets),
`field-flanks`, `field-wide`, `field-raids`, `field-night` (night raid), `field-dawn`, `field-order`,
`field-advance`, `field-crush` (compression), `field-sweep` (tulughma), `field-trap`. Keep camera moves slow
and motivated by the narration; cut wide → close → wide.
