# 12. Originality and monetisation

A pipeline that makes the next film "mostly a script-writing job" is exactly what YouTube's
**inauthentic content** policy watches for. Read this before planning a series, and check every film against
it before upload.

## 12.1 The policy (YouTube Partner Program, checked October 2026)

Source: YouTube channel monetisation policies, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392

- Since **15 July 2025** the old "repetitious content" rule is called **inauthentic content**: channels whose
  videos are repetitive or mass-produced are **not eligible for the Partner Program** (no ad revenue).
  It is judged mostly at the **channel level** (when applying for monetisation and in later reviews), not
  as per-video demonetisation.
- Not allowed (YouTube's examples): content "that looks like it's made with a template"; "image slideshows,
  templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative"; "AI-generated content made with
  generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production".
- **Reused content** rule, also relevant: not allowed is content that "exclusively features readings of other
  materials you did not originally create". Primary-source quotes must support our own explanation, never be
  the content.
- Explicitly fine: the same intro and outro when the bulk of each video is different; "a series" across
  episodes; AI used as a creative tool where the creator's vision is evident.
- Third-party reports (not YouTube's wording) describe 2026 enforcement against channels that combine
  synthetic narration, templated thumbnails, stock-footage loops and an upload pace no human editorial
  process could sustain.
- Separate from this: the **AI use** disclosure at upload (reference 11.5) must be Yes for our films. It
  labels the video; it does not by itself affect monetisation.

## 12.2 Our risk profile

| Signal | Atlas of Time | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Original research and script per film | Fact table, primary sources, our own argument | Strong |
| On-screen and description sources | Yes | Strong |
| Upload pace | One film every 2–3 weeks, Shorts in between | Fine |
| Narration | Kokoro `af_heart`: a free stock voice many AI channels use | **Risk** (the person chose to keep it; offset with the items below) |
| Visual template | Reused shots, cards and battle scenes across films | **Risk if unmanaged** |
| Thumbnails | One fixed layout with new numbers | **Risk if unmanaged** |

## 12.3 Rules for every film (definition of done before upload)

1. **New 3D set pieces for each battle.** Rebuild the battle around that battle's own tactics and terrain
   (Khanwa: the chained carts and wheeled tripods, the lake beside the camp, the Rajput charges), not
   Panipat's scenes with new labels. Reuse models and systems, not shots.
2. **New maps and routes, new paintings and photographs** for each film. At least half the visual ids `v` in
   `script.json` must be new to this film.
3. **Reuse only the brand frame:** title card, chapter-card style, typography, end card, watermark.
4. **Thumbnails:** keep the brand look (palette, type), but vary the composition per film (subject, framing,
   text placement). Never a fill-in-the-numbers template.
5. **Quotes are a minority of the narration**, under about 15% of the words, each one set up and followed by
   our own explanation.
6. **Voice:** one consistent narrator (Kokoro `af_heart`, by the person's choice). A second Kokoro voice
   (e.g. `bm_george`) may read primary-source quotes as a character voice, for production value; decide at
   script review. Do not rotate narrators between films: it doesn't reduce the synthetic-voice signal and
   it weakens the brand.
7. **Shorts are distinct cuts** (different moments, captions and hooks), 2–3 per film, never near-identical
   clips and never in bulk.
8. **Pace:** no more than one long film a week; a 2–3 week cadence is the default.
9. **Description:** sources, notes on disputed numbers, credits and the AI note, written for this film.

## 12.4 Before applying for monetisation

Thresholds change, so check the current YouTube Partner Program requirements. When applying:
- Look at the channel page the way a reviewer would: do the last 6–10 videos look made by hand, with
  different visuals, thumbnails and topics?
- Make sure every video has the AI disclosure set and its description sources.
- Remove or rework anything that looks like a template fill (e.g. a Short that repeats another).
